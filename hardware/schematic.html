Classroom Heat Monitor: hardware schematic

ESP32 reads a DHT22 once per wake-up, shows the result on an OLED for a few seconds, sends it over Wi-Fi, then deep-sleeps. Two 18650 cells in series feed a 5 V buck converter through a protection board.

Status: drafted, not yet built or measured on real hardware.

Read before you wire anything
  • Never connect the two cells in series until you have measured each one. Both must be 3.6–4.2 V and within 0.1 V of each other, same brand, same capacity, same age.
  • Set the buck converter to its output voltage with nothing connected to its output, then switch off and wire it to the ESP32.
  • Your ESP32 board's pin positions may differ from other 38-pin boards. Match the GPIO numbers printed on the board, not the position in this drawing.
  • Charge the cells outside the device, one at a time, in a normal single-cell 18650 charger. There is no charging circuit in this design on purpose.

Bring-up order

  1. Measure each cell. Both 3.6–4.2 V, within 0.1 V.
  2. Wire the BMS per its sheet: B− first, then BM, then B+ last. Measure across P+ and P−: you should see 7.2–8.4 V.
  3. Fit F1 and SW1 with the switch off. Connect U1 input only. Switch on and trim the output to 5.20 V with a multimeter. Switch off.
  4. Add D1, C1, C2 and the 5V/VIN and GND wires. Switch on. At the ESP32 VIN pin you should read about 4.8–5.0 V, and the 3V3 pin should read 3.3 V. Fix this before adding anything else.
  5. Switch off. Add the DHT22, OLED, button and divider. Switch on and check the divider midpoint reads about 2.3–2.7 V on a fresh pack.
  6. Flash the firmware over USB with SW1 off.

The limit is the devkit itself: its voltage regulator and USB chip draw 5–10 mA even in deep sleep. A low-power ESP32 board (about 10 µA asleep) would stretch this to months. Measure real sleep current with a multimeter in series before trusting any of these numbers.

Open items

  • The classroom has no Wi-Fi. This schematic only gets the device powered and measuring. For readings to reach a public web app, something in or near the room must supply internet: a pocket 4G router or phone hotspot (ESP32 stays as drawn), or a cellular module on the device (changes this schematic). Needs your decision.
  • Cell type, buck module model and ESP32 board variant are assumed. Send photos or part names if they differ.
  • Heat index is only meaningful above about 27 °C and 40 % RH. The DHT22 is accurate to about ±0.5 °C and ±2–5 % RH, so the displayed value is an estimate.
