Classroom Heat Monitor: hardware schematic

ESP32 reads a DHT22 once per wake-up, shows the result on an OLED for a few seconds, sends it over Wi-Fi, then deep-sleeps. A USB power bank feeds the board's USB port directly: no battery pack, protection board or converter to build.

Status: drafted, not yet built or measured on real hardware. Rev B replaces Rev A (2S 18650 cells + buck converter).

Read before you wire anything
  • The power bank may switch itself off when the load is tiny (deep sleep draws only about 10 mA). Test this first, with the bare board (bring-up step 1), before building the rest.
  • Use a data-capable USB cable, USB-A on the power bank side. Flash from the PC first, then move the cable to the power bank: one USB source at a time.
  • Your ESP32 board's pin positions may differ from other ESP32 boards. Match the GPIO numbers printed on the board, not the position in this drawing.
  • Keep the power bank out of direct sun and away from the DHT22: a warm bank heats the sensor reading, and Li-ion packs should not sit above about 45 °C.

Bring-up order

  • Power bank test first. Plug the bare ESP32 board into the power bank with an inline USB meter. Note the idle current and leave it for an hour: does the bank stay on? A bank that switches off at low current can't be used with deep sleep (see Runtime estimate).
  • With the board powered from the bank, measure the 3V3 pin: it should read 3.3 V.
  • Unplug USB. Add the DHT22, OLED, button and the 3V3 / GND wires per the wire list.
  • Flash the firmware from the PC over USB, then move the cable to the power bank. One USB source at a time.
  • Run it for a day on the bank and compare the measured drop with the estimate.

    • Runtime estimate

    Rough figures from datasheet-class numbers, not measured on your build. The 30 000 mAh label means 111 Wh in the cells; after boost losses expect about 18 000 mAh at 5 V, and less if the bank is warm or old. This assumes the bank stays switched on at the tiny current of deep sleep, which must be tested.

    ModeAverage drawEstimated life (18 000 mAh at 5 V)

    The devkit's regulator and USB chip draw most of the sleep current (about 8–10 mA). A low-power ESP32 board would stretch this further. Measure real current with an inline USB meter before trusting any number here.

    Open items

    • The classroom has no Wi-Fi. The device needs a pocket 4G router or phone hotspot nearby (D-004). The ESP32 stays a Wi-Fi client.
    • Power bank auto-off is untested. The ROMOSS Sense 8P+ must be tested with the bare board at low load (bring-up step 1) before anything else is built. Look in its manual for a low-current mode.
    • Cable: the bank has a USB-A port and your board is Type-C, so use a USB-A to Type-C data cable. Avoid C-to-C with this bank: it may try to negotiate 9 V / 12 V, and a board without proper CC resistors gets no power at all.
    • Heat index is only meaningful above about 27 °C and 40 % RH. The DHT22 is accurate to about ±0.5 °C and ±2–5 % RH, so the displayed value is an estimate.
